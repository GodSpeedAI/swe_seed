What does the gate actually detect?

The trace ledger is a SHA-256 chain: each entry commits to the previous entry's stored hash. Two edits produce two different failures, and one kind of tampering goes undetected. Step through a real four-entry chain and apply each edit.

gate: allow

Not modelled here: truncation. Each append records a head anchor in ledger.anchors.json. Removing the last row of an anchored chain makes gate --verify fail with head anchor mismatch, and appending after that is refused before the anchor is overwritten. A trace with no anchor fails as unanchored unless --allow-unanchored is passed.

Provenance. The four entries were produced in a throwaway root by trace start, trace append, trace checkpoint, and trace finish. The gate wording and both block messages were captured from the binary. The truncation rule comes from verify_chain_with() and the append guard; it was not re-captured from the binary.
